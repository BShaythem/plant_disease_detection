# Disease card — Olive bud mite (Aculus olearius)

## Identification

- **Name (EN)**: Olive bud mite (erineum / rust mite damage)
- **Scientific name**: Aculus olearius
- **Pathogen type**: Mite (eriophyid)

## Symptoms

Feeds on succulent stem and bud tissue and on the upper leaf surface; damage includes sickle-shaped leaves, dead vegetative buds in spring, discoloration of flower buds, bud drop, and reduced shoot growth; typical silvery-to-bronze leaf appearance caused by eriophyid mites.

*Source: UC IPM (University of California) Pest Management Guidelines*

## Favorable conditions

Develops mainly in spring (peak in May) and to a lesser extent in autumn; feeds mainly on young shoots, so the period of strongest tree growth is most affected.

*Source: Excelentesprecios (Spanish agricultural technical resource)*

## Severity stages

| Stage | Description | Urgency |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A few young shoots with slight silvering | Monitoring |
| 2 | Clear bronzing of young leaves, mild deformation | Treatment recommended |
| 3 | Visible erineum (felting), slowed shoot growth | Urgent treatment |

*Source: compiled by synthesis from UC IPM and Excelentesprecios — not from a single official source. TO BE VALIDATED BY AN AGRONOMIST.*

## Treatment

### Acaricide / wettable sulfur specific to eriophyid mites
- **Dosage**: to confirm per commercial product and local regulations
- **Frequency**: treatment carried out during flowering to prevent fruit deformation; or early spring (March) / autumn if symptoms are detected
- **Precautions**: target young shoots; rotate active ingredients to avoid resistance; respect the pre-harvest interval

*Source: Excelentesprecios (sulfur/abamectin); UC IPM notes this pest is generally NOT managed unless yield/fruit set has been below normal for several consecutive years.*

### Encourage natural predators (predatory mites)
- **Dosage**: not applicable
- **Frequency**: ongoing, preventive measure
- **Precautions**: avoid broad-spectrum treatments that destroy natural predators

*Source: general principle of biological control against eriophyid mites (UC IPM)*
